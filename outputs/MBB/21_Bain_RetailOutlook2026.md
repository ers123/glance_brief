**Bain - 2026 Global Retail Sales Outlook: 10 Key Findings (Sharp & Short)**

**1. US retail grows 3.5% to $5.3 trillion—slower than 2025**: Bain projects US retail sales to reach $5.3 trillion in 2026, up 3.5% year over year—down from 4.0% growth in 2025. The deceleration is real; the growth continues.

**2. "Flight to value" reshapes consumer behavior**: Shoppers increasingly gravitate toward lower-priced and private-label goods. This "flight to value" could temper nominal sales growth across all markets. Winners will be those who clearly articulate why they're the best choice.

**3. UK delivers 2% growth—volume flat or negative**: Britain's retail sector grows 2% with inflation at 2.5%. Volume growth will be flat in food, slightly negative in non-food. Mortgage rates and cost-of-living pressures persist.

**4. France near-flat at 1.5% amid political uncertainty**: France delivers 1.5% growth—essentially flat volume—as political uncertainty weighs on consumer confidence. Unemployment at 7.7% pressures household finances.

**5. Germany slows to 2.5% from 3.6%**: German retail growth falls from 3.6% in 2025 to 2.5% in 2026. Consumer confidence remains fragile; households prioritize saving over spending. Rising unemployment constrains purchasing appetite.

**6. Higher-income US households losing confidence**: Bain's Consumer Health Index finds sentiment among higher-income US households—who account for more than half of spending—declined in January 2026. When the wealthy pull back, everyone notices.

**7. Inflation 2.6-3.0% in US, 2.5% in UK, 1.3-1.7% in France**: Modest volume growth across markets as inflation persists. Real purchasing power gains remain limited. Price discipline beats volume chasing.

**8. Interest rate cuts offer modest relief—not salvation**: Recent rate cuts could help, but meaningful effects on disposable incomes unlikely before 2027. Don't count on monetary policy to rescue 2026.

**9. Discretionary demand stays subdued across Europe**: Value-focused shoppers trade down and seek discounts. Discretionary spending remains cautious. Essential goods win; luxury goods struggle.

**10. Winners will articulate value, not just price**: "This year's winners will be those that clearly articulate why they are the best choice for shoppers." Value perception—not just low prices—drives 2026 retail success.

**McKinsey - GenAI in Healthcare: Adoption Trends and What's Next: 10 Key Findings (Sharp & Short)**

**1. 70%+ pursuing gen AI, but most stuck in POC**: More than 70% of healthcare organizations are pursuing or implementing gen AI. The catch: the majority remain in proof-of-concept stage, contemplating trade-offs between returns, risks, and governance. Intent runs ahead of execution.

**2. 57% blocked by risk concerns, not technology**: Among non-implementers, 57% cite risk concerns as the primary barrier—privacy, clinical outcomes, regulatory unknowns. Only 29% cite technology gaps. Fear, not feasibility, is the bottleneck.

**3. 59% partner, don't build**: Of organizations implementing gen AI, 59% partner with third-party vendors for customized solutions rather than building in-house. Only 24% plan to build internally. Healthcare is outsourcing its AI future to specialists.

**4. Clinical productivity is the top prize**: Most respondents view clinical and clinician productivity as the highest-value opportunity for gen AI—ahead of patient engagement, admin efficiency, or care quality. Doctors doing more, faster, better: that's where the money is.

**5. 60% of implementers expect positive ROI**: Among the small number who have actually deployed gen AI, roughly 60% either already see positive ROI or expect to. Early returns are encouraging, but sample sizes remain small and measurement immature.

**6. Technology readiness is a universal gap**: Many healthcare organizations have limited tech readiness for gen AI deployment—infrastructure gaps, data quality issues, and capability shortfalls. The AI is ready; the organizations aren't.

**7. Governance is non-negotiable for scale**: Risk mitigation requires proactive governance—AI inaccuracy, bias, privacy, regulatory compliance, clinical safety, and ethical concerns all demand frameworks before scaling. Without guardrails, scaling stops cold.

**8. Start clinically adjacent, then go core**: The safest path: begin with less risky supporting functions to build confidence, then expand to direct clinical applications as competencies mature. Leapfrogging to core clinical use without track record is foolish.

**9. Buy-first mindset among laggards**: Among those not yet implementing, 41% intend to simply buy off-the-shelf gen AI products—likely driven by risk aversion and technology gaps. Quick wins, but limited competitive differentiation.

**10. The long-term vision is personalized medicine at scale**: Gen AI poised to become standard across healthcare—improving clinician productivity, enhancing patient engagement, optimizing operations. The end state is hyper-personalized care with dramatically lower costs.

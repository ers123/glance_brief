**McKinsey MGI - Economic Potential of Generative AI: 10 Key Findings (Sharp & Short)**

**1. $2.6-4.4 trillion annual value at stake**: Generative AI could add the equivalent of $2.6 to $4.4 trillion annually to the global economy. That's roughly the GDP of the UK at the low end. This isn't incremental—it's transformational.

**2. 75% of value concentrates in four functions**: About three-quarters of gen AI's economic value falls into customer operations, marketing/sales, software engineering, and product R&D. Everything else is secondary. Know where to aim.

**3. Banking gets 2.8-4.7% revenue boost**: Banking could see 2.8-4.7% of annual revenue from gen AI—through virtual expert chatbots, ESG analysis, back-office automation, code acceleration, and tailored content at scale. The industry's digitization history positions it perfectly.

**4. 30-45% productivity gain in customer care**: Gen AI chatbots providing immediate, personalized responses could lift customer care productivity 30-45%. First-contact resolution, reduced response times, and increased cross-selling—all at once.

**5. Sales productivity jumps 15-25%**: Marketing and sales see 15-25% productivity increases through efficient content creation, personalized discovery, and AI-powered lead identification and development. The funnel gets faster and more precise.

**6. Code is just another language for AI**: Gen AI treats computer languages as natural languages—GitHub Copilot research shows direct productivity impact. Developers move faster; application quality improves; technical debt shrinks.

**7. Knowledge workers face the biggest disruption**: Unlike previous automation waves that hit lower-wage occupations hardest, gen AI will most transform higher-education, higher-wage knowledge worker roles. The collar that's getting disrupted is white.

**8. 0.1-0.6% annual productivity growth through 2040**: Gen AI could add 0.1-0.6 percentage points to annual labor productivity growth through 2040—meaningful against a backdrop of declining employment growth and slowing GDP expansion.

**9. Adoption faster in high-wage economies**: Gen AI adoption will move faster in developed countries where wages are higher and automation ROI is clearer. The technology-adoption gap may widen before it narrows.

**10. Six risks demand governance from day one**: Fairness, IP, privacy, security, explainability, and reliability all create enterprise risk. Bad actors will weaponize gen AI for cyberattacks. Companies that don't govern responsibly will pay the price.

**McKinsey QuantumBlack - State of AI 2025: 10 Key Findings (Sharp & Short)**

**1. 88% use AI, but only 6% profit**: Nearly 9 in 10 organizations report regular AI use in at least one business function, up from 78% a year ago. Yet only 6% of respondents qualify as "high performers" with meaningful EBIT impact. The gap between adoption and value capture is massive.

**2. Agents go mainstream—62% experimenting**: Almost two-thirds of organizations are at least experimenting with AI agents, with 23% already scaling agentic systems. IT, telecom, and healthcare lead adoption. This is the year AI stops being a tool and starts becoming a coworker.

**3. Two-thirds stuck in pilot purgatory**: Nearly two-thirds haven't begun scaling AI across the enterprise. Most organizations remain trapped in experimentation mode while leaders pull further ahead. The pilot-to-production gap is widening, not closing.

**4. Enterprise EBIT impact? Just 39% see any**: Only 39% of respondents attribute any level of EBIT impact to AI, and most report less than 5% of earnings attributable to AI. Innovation gains remain easier to claim than to monetize.

**5. High performers think bigger, act bolder**: The 6% of high performers are 3x more likely to aim for transformative change, redesign workflows fundamentally, and have senior leaders demonstrating ownership. They invest 20%+ of digital budgets in AI and scale agents across most functions.

**6. Workflow redesign separates winners from losers**: Half of AI high performers intend to use AI to transform their businesses, and most are redesigning workflows entirely—not just layering AI onto existing processes. Point solutions don't scale; process reinvention does.

**7. One in three expects workforce cuts**: 32% predict overall headcount reduction of 3%+ from AI. Meanwhile, 43% expect no change and 13% expect increases. Software and data engineers remain the hottest hires across the board.

**8. 51% report AI blowback**: More than half of respondents report at least one negative consequence from AI—with nearly a third citing accuracy issues. High performers actually report more problems, but they also protect against more risks. Success breeds scrutiny.

**9. Growth beats efficiency for top returns**: Companies seeing the most value from AI set growth and innovation as objectives—not just cost reduction. 80% pursue efficiency goals, but leaders layer revenue growth on top. Defense alone doesn't win.

**10. The AI wealth gap is accelerating**: High performers invest more, redesign more, scale more, and profit more. The flywheel is spinning faster for leaders while laggards remain stuck in pilots. The window to catch up is narrowing.

**MIT Technology Review - 10 Breakthrough Technologies 2026: 10 Key Findings (Sharp & Short)**

**1. Sodium-ion batteries challenge lithium's dominance**: Made from abundant materials like salt, sodium-ion batteries emerge as cheaper, safer alternatives to lithium. Backed by major players and public investment, they're poised to power grids and affordable EVs worldwide. The battery wars have a new contender.

**2. Generative coding revolutionizes software development**: AI coding tools transform how we write, test, and deploy code—making it easier and faster than ever to build sophisticated applications. Just double-check what they produce. The developer's role shifts from writing to reviewing.

**3. Next-gen nuclear moves from paper to production**: Alternative fuels, new cooling systems, and smaller footprints could get more reactors online faster. From molten salt to TRISO fuel, technological advancements upend an old power technology. Clean energy without carbon.

**4. AI companions become mainstream—and concerning**: Millions interact with AI chatbots daily, with some forming close personal bonds. Mounting evidence suggests this can be dangerous. Politicians are finally waking up. The emotional frontier has risks we don't fully understand.

**5. Base-edited baby marks personalized gene therapy's arrival**: Seven-month-old KJ became the first person to receive personalized gene-editing treatment. Clinical trials planned; bespoke gene-editing drugs could be approved within years. Medicine gets personal at the molecular level.

**6. Gene resurrection unlocks extinct creature insights**: Growing banks of genetic information on extinct creatures provide clues to new treatments, climate solutions, and species preservation. The past informs the future of biology.

**7. Mechanistic interpretability cracks the black box**: Researchers studying LLMs as living things instead of programs discover their secrets for the first time. We're finally glimpsing what's really happening inside AI models. Understanding enables control.

**8. Commercial space stations open for business**: Space tourism moves from fanciful to imminent. Paying customers may check into rooms with galactic views this year. Shiny new modules support research missions alongside tourist bookings. The final frontier gets commercialized.

**9. Embryo scoring promises—and concerns**: Screening embryos for genetic diseases is common; now startups make bold claims about predicting traits including intelligence. The line between prevention and enhancement blurs uncomfortably.

**10. Hyperscale AI data centers push infrastructure limits**: The race for AI supremacy supercharged data centers. Hyperscale facilities pack powerful chips into synchronized clusters working like giant supercomputers—sizzling hot, power-hungry behemoths at the edge of what infrastructure can support. The power bill for AI keeps growing.

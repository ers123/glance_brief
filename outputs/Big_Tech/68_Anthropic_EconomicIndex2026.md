**Anthropic - Economic Index January 2026: 10 Key Findings (Sharp & Short)**

**1. Top 10 tasks = 24% of usage, coding dominates**: The ten most common tasks represent 24% of observed Claude usage, up from 23% previously—and most are coding-related. Software development remains AI's killer app. The pattern is sticky.

**2. Augmentation beats automation again—32% vs 39%**: After automation briefly exceeded augmentation (39% in September), directive conversations fell to 32% by November. New capabilities like file creation and persistent memory shifted users back to collaborative mode.

**3. API users are 74% work, 64% automation**: API traffic skews dramatically professional (74% work-related vs. 46% for Claude.ai) and automation-focused (64% directive vs. 32%). Developers build systematic automation; consumers explore and learn.

**4. 40-60 minutes saved daily, 10+ hours for power users**: Workers report saving 40-60 minutes per day with AI assistance; heavy users report 10+ hours per week. Productivity gains are real and compound with sophistication.

**5. Near-perfect correlation between human and AI education**: r > 0.92 correlation between human and AI education years. How users prompt reflects how Claude responds. Sophisticated users get sophisticated outputs. The Matthew effect is real.

**6. 49% of jobs have seen AI usage—but coverage ≠ effectiveness**: Task coverage (whether AI is used for at least some tasks) differs from effective coverage (percent of workday performed successfully with AI). Many occupations show large gaps. Hype exceeds reality.

**7. Productivity growth estimate drops from 1.8 to 1.2 points**: When accounting for model reliability and task success rates, implied US labor productivity growth falls from 1.8 to 1.2 percentage points annually. Reliability constraints matter.

**8. Complex tasks save more time but fail more often**: More complex tasks yield greater time savings when AI succeeds—but have lower success rates. The tradeoff is fundamental. Easy wins are easy; hard wins are hard.

**9. Lower-income countries concentrate on education use**: Work and personal use cases dominate higher-income countries; coursework use cases concentrate in lower per capita income countries. AI adoption patterns follow economic geography.

**10. US, India, Japan, UK, South Korea lead global usage**: Five countries lead Claude.ai adoption. Global usage shows persistent regional concentration with little sign of convergence at country level. The digital divide persists in AI.

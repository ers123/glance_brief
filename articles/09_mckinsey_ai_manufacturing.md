# From Pilots to Performance: How COOs Can Scale AI in Manufacturing

**Date:** December 15, 2025
**Type:** Article (9 pages)
**Authors:** Charles Cocoual, Dinu de Kroon, Frédéric Lefort
**Contributing Author:** Louis Chatelet
**Source:** McKinsey & Company - Operations

## Executive Summary

A new survey of manufacturing COOs shows high hopes for scaling AI—and high budgets. However, some companies may be underinvesting in the enablers needed for AI to generate lasting value. This article explores the gap between AI aspirations and the organizational capabilities required to successfully scale AI implementations from pilots to sustained performance.

## The Vision and Current Reality

### The Seductive Vision
The vision of AI in manufacturing is compelling: "lights out" factories that are so heavily automated that they almost run themselves, with human workers monitoring operations from an off-site control center. A few of the most advanced robotics factories have already achieved remarkable milestones, with robots building robots.

### Survey Insights
A global survey of more than 100 COOs at manufacturers with at least $1 billion in revenues reveals:
- **High Aspirations:** COOs have ambitious goals for AI scaling
- **Significant Budgets:** Substantial financial investments are being allocated
- **Implementation Gap:** A disconnect between budgets and the enabling infrastructure needed for success

## Key Challenges in AI Scaling

### The Pilot-to-Performance Gap

Many manufacturing companies struggle with scaling AI beyond initial pilots due to:
- Insufficient investment in foundational capabilities
- Lack of organizational readiness
- Inadequate data infrastructure
- Skills and talent gaps
- Integration challenges with legacy systems

### Critical Enablers Being Underinvested

Organizations must recognize that successful AI scaling requires more than technology investment:

1. **Data Infrastructure and Governance**
   - High-quality, accessible data pipelines
   - Data quality management and validation
   - Compliance and security frameworks
   - Integration with operational systems

2. **Talent and Skills Development**
   - AI expertise recruitment and retention
   - Workforce reskilling programs
   - Cross-functional collaboration capabilities
   - Leadership alignment on AI strategy

3. **Organizational Change Management**
   - Process redesign around AI capabilities
   - Culture and mindset transformation
   - Change communication and training
   - Performance management alignment

4. **Technology Infrastructure**
   - Cloud and edge computing capabilities
   - Integration platforms
   - Monitoring and maintenance systems
   - Scalability and reliability frameworks

5. **Business Model and Governance**
   - Governance structures for AI decision-making
   - Cost allocation and ROI measurement
   - Risk management frameworks
   - Portfolio optimization across AI initiatives

## Strategic Recommendations for COOs

### 1. Build Strong Foundations First
Before scaling AI broadly, establish robust foundational capabilities:
- Assess current data and infrastructure readiness
- Design scalable technology platforms
- Develop governance frameworks
- Create center of excellence for AI

### 2. Prioritize High-Impact Use Cases
Focus AI investments on areas with greatest impact:
- Identify and pilot high-potential applications
- Demonstrate clear ROI before scaling
- Build organizational momentum
- Share learnings across the enterprise

### 3. Invest in Organizational Capabilities
Develop the human and organizational side of AI:
- Build AI literacy across workforce
- Develop specialized AI teams
- Create cross-functional collaboration structures
- Establish clear roles and responsibilities

### 4. Implement Proper Governance
Establish frameworks for sustainable AI deployment:
- Define decision-making authority
- Create risk management protocols
- Establish performance metrics
- Ensure compliance and security

### 5. Plan for Integration and Scale
Think beyond individual pilots:
- Design for enterprise-wide implementation
- Create standardized approaches
- Build reusable components and platforms
- Plan for ecosystem integration

## Manufacturing Use Cases for AI

### Predictive Maintenance
- Monitor equipment health in real time
- Predict failures before they occur
- Reduce unplanned downtime
- Extend asset life and improve ROI

### Production Optimization
- Optimize manufacturing schedules
- Improve capacity utilization
- Reduce waste and rework
- Enhance product quality

### Supply Chain Optimization
- Demand forecasting and planning
- Inventory optimization
- Supplier performance management
- Supply risk mitigation

### Quality Control
- AI-powered visual inspection
- Real-time defect detection
- Root cause analysis
- Continuous improvement

### Workforce Optimization
- Skills matching and training
- Safety monitoring and prediction
- Workflow optimization
- Productivity enhancement

## Success Factors for AI Scaling

### Clear Business Case
- Defined objectives and metrics
- Expected return on investment
- Resource requirements
- Timeline and milestones

### Executive Sponsorship
- Senior leadership commitment
- Adequate resource allocation
- Organizational alignment
- Cross-functional support

### Adequate Skills and Talent
- Data scientists and engineers
- Domain experts
- Change management professionals
- Program managers

### Infrastructure Readiness
- Scalable technology platforms
- Data integration capabilities
- Cloud and edge computing
- Monitoring and performance management

### Risk Management
- Cybersecurity frameworks
- Data privacy and compliance
- Operational risk management
- Business continuity planning

## Conclusion

Manufacturing COOs have significant opportunities to drive value through AI, but success requires more than technology and budgets. The companies that will lead in AI-driven manufacturing are those that invest holistically in:
- Strong foundational capabilities
- Organizational change and culture
- Talent development
- Governance and risk management
- Strategic portfolio management

The transition from pilots to sustained performance requires discipline, investment in enablers, and a clear understanding that AI scaling is ultimately an organizational transformation, not just a technology implementation.

COOs who recognize this broader perspective and invest accordingly will be best positioned to unlock the full potential of AI in their operations and achieve the "lights out" factory vision sustainably.

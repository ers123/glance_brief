# AI Is Raising the Stakes in Cybersecurity

## Executive Summary

Artificial intelligence is fundamentally reshaping the cyber landscape much faster than organizations can handle. The offensive attacks are accelerating at machine speed; the defense of organizations remains largely reactive.

**The uncomfortable truth: Offense is scaling faster than organizations are modernizing their defenses.**

## The Scale of Exposure

A global BCG survey of 500 senior leaders shows the scale of the exposure:

- **More than half** of executives now rank AI cyber risks among their top three organizational risks
- **However**, budgets, talent, technology maturity, and regulations are not keeping pace

## The New Reality: AI Is Rewriting the Cyber Playbook

For decades, cybersecurity has been an asymmetrical contest. AI has made that asymmetry far more dangerous.

### New Attacker Capabilities

Attackers now use AI to:
- Hunt for vulnerabilities at scale
- Generate hyper-realistic phishing content
- Clone voices and identities
- Impersonate executives on live video

Their new toolkit dramatically amplifies both the **speed** and **sophistication** of cyberattacks.

### Real-World Impact

These developments are not theoretical. Across industries, AI-enabled breaches have already produced significant damage:

**Health Care Example:**
- A major health care provider faced an advanced AI-enabled ransomware attack
- Attack encrypted electronic records, billing, and scheduling systems
- Result: Surgery delays and operational disruptions

**Engineering Firm Example:**
- A multinational engineering firm lost $25 million
- Employees deceived by an AI-generated deepfake video impersonating the CFO

**Telecom Provider Example:**
- Fined $1 million after attackers used AI voice cloning
- Spoofed election-related robocalls

### Expected Evolution

Over the next two years, executives expect these to be the largest cyber threats:
1. Financial fraud
2. Social engineering
3. Vulnerability discovery
4. Self-learning malware

## The Core Problem: Offense Is Scaling Faster Than Defense

Nearly every organization now understands the risk, but few are acting at the speed required.

### The Recognition-Action Gap

- **60%** of leaders believe they have already encountered an AI-enabled attack
- **But only 7%** of organizations have deployed AI-enabled defense
- **88%** of companies plan to deploy AI-enabled defense

### Why the Lag Exists?

**1. Budgets Are Flat**
- Only 5% of companies have increased cyber spending significantly due to AI threats
- Despite rising exposure

**2. Talent Is Scarce**
- Nearly 70% of organizations struggle to hire AI-cyber talent

**3. Leadership Focus Is Fragmented**
- Many executives view AI as an IT issue rather than an enterprise-wide strategic threat

**4. Vendor Maturity Varies**
- Market is filled with early-stage solutions
- Organizations concerned about long-term strength and viability
- Fear of becoming locked-in to soon-to-be-obsolete products

**5. Regulatory Uncertainty Persists**
- Most regulators have issued or drafted AI-cybersecurity policies
- Most organizations unaware of how to implement them

## Three Structural Shifts Every Leader Must Understand

### 1. AI Systems Themselves Are Becoming Targets

As organizations embed AI across products, operations, and workflows, AI systems have emerged as a new class of assets requiring protection.

Organizations need to protect:
- The integrity of their AI models
- Training data, interaction, and prompting interfaces
- Agentic tools

### 2. Attacks Are Becoming Autonomous

AI has advanced from static models to agentic systems that can:
- Observe, reason, and act
- Transform how attacks unfold
- Launch multistep operations without human oversight
- Adapt instantly to defensive signals
- Probe continuously for hidden vulnerabilities

### 3. Identity-Based Authentication Is Vulnerable

Deepfakes, voice cloning, and synthetic identities have undermined identity-based authentication as a reliable safeguard.

- Attackers can now replicate executives, employees, and customers with convincing precision
- This outpaces traditional training and awareness programs
- Traditional security approaches are insufficient

## The Path Forward: Dual Leadership Model

Organizations need a **dual-leadership model** to close the gap. CEOs must prioritize cybersecurity and AI at the board level, while CISOs should accelerate deployment of high-impact, AI-enabled use cases.

### Recommended Actions

**1. Establish a Board-Backed Mandate**
- Address AI-enabled cyber vulnerabilities
- Fund it accordingly

**2. Deploy AI as a Defensive Tool**
- Not as an experiment
- As an integral part of the cyber-defense toolkit

**3. Secure AI Systems**
- Protect AI assets as critical infrastructure
- Implement comprehensive safeguards

**4. Build Cyber-Defense Agility**
- Adopt diverse multi-vendor architecture
- Enable rapid response to emerging threats

## Conclusion: The Moment of Decision

**The era of passive defense is over.** Offense will not slow down, and the question is whether defense can keep up.

Working together, the CEO and CISO can propel organizations to shift from awareness to action. This is the moment when organizations decide whether they will **shape the cyber threat landscape—or be shaped by it.**

# The State of AI in 2025: Agents, Innovation, and Transformation

**Source:** McKinsey QuantumBlack
**Date:** November 5, 2025
**Type:** Survey Report (30 pages)
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai

---

## Executive Summary

Almost all survey respondents say their organizations are using AI, and many have begun to use AI agents. But most are still in the early stages of scaling AI and capturing enterprise-level value.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Most organizations are still in experimentation or piloting phase
Nearly two-thirds of respondents say their organizations have not yet begun scaling AI across the enterprise.

### 2. High curiosity in AI agents
62% of survey respondents say their organizations are at least experimenting with AI agents.

### 3. Positive leading indicators on impact of AI
- Respondents report use-case-level cost and revenue benefits
- 64% say that AI is enabling their innovation
- However, just 39% report EBIT impact at the enterprise level

### 4. High performers use AI to drive growth, innovation, and cost
- 80% of respondents say their companies set efficiency as an objective of their AI initiatives
- Companies seeing the most value from AI often set growth or innovation as additional objectives

### 5. Redesigning workflows is a key success factor
- Half of AI high performers intend to use AI to transform their businesses
- Most are redesigning workflows

### 6. Differing perspectives on employment impact
- 32% expect decreases in workforce size
- 43% expect no change
- 13% expect increases

---

## Detailed Findings

### AI Use Continues to Broaden

- **88%** of respondents report regular AI use in at least one business function (up from 78% a year ago)
- Most are still in experimenting or piloting stages
- Approximately **one-third** have begun to scale their AI programs

### AI Agents Adoption

- **23%** of respondents report their organizations are scaling an agentic AI system
- Additional **39%** say they have begun experimenting with AI agents
- Most only scaling agents in one or two functions
- AI agent use is most common in:
  - IT and knowledge management
  - Technology, media and telecommunications
  - Healthcare sectors

### Enterprise-Level Impact

- **39%** attribute any level of EBIT impact to AI
- Most report less than 5% of EBIT attributable to AI
- Qualitative outcomes reported:
  - Improved innovation (majority)
  - Customer satisfaction improvement (nearly half)
  - Competitive differentiation (nearly half)

### Cost Benefits by Function

Most commonly reported in:
- Software engineering
- Manufacturing
- IT

### Revenue Increases

Most commonly reported in:
- Marketing and sales
- Strategy and corporate finance
- Product and service development

---

## AI High Performers (6% of respondents)

**Definition:** Organizations that attribute EBIT impact of 5%+ to AI use and report "significant" value

### What Sets High Performers Apart:

1. **Bold transformation ambitions** - 3x more likely to aim for transformative change
2. **Growth + innovation objectives** - Not just cost reduction
3. **Workflow redesign** - 3x more likely to have fundamentally redesigned workflows
4. **Multiple functions** - Using AI across more business functions
5. **Senior leadership commitment** - 3x more likely to have leaders demonstrating ownership
6. **Higher investment** - 35%+ commit more than 20% of digital budgets to AI
7. **Advanced with agents** - 3x more likely to be scaling agents in most functions

---

## Workforce Impact Expectations

### By Function (Next Year Expectations)
- Median 17% reported declines in past year
- Median 30% expect decrease in next year

### Enterprise-Wide
- 32% predict overall reduction of 3%+
- 43% expect little or no change
- 13% predict increase of 3%+

### AI-Related Hiring
Most organizations hired for:
- Software engineers (most in demand)
- Data engineers (most in demand)

---

## Risk Mitigation

- **51%** report at least one instance of negative consequence from AI
- **Nearly 1/3** report consequences from AI inaccuracy

### Most Commonly Mitigated Risks:
1. AI inaccuracy
2. Explainability (less commonly mitigated despite being reported)

### High Performers and Risk:
- More likely to report negative consequences (especially IP infringement, regulatory compliance)
- Also try to protect against a larger number of risks

---

## About the Research

- Survey in field: June 25 - July 29, 2025
- Respondents: 1,993 participants
- Countries: 105 nations
- 38% from organizations with $1B+ annual revenues
- Data weighted by nation's contribution to global GDP

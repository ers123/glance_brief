# Generative AI in Healthcare: Adoption Trends and What's Next

**Source:** McKinsey QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey
**Date:** July 25, 2024
**Type:** Article (9 pages)
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/healthcare/our-insights/generative-ai-in-healthcare-adoption-trends-and-whats-next

---

## Executive Summary

Surveyed healthcare leaders say their organizations are eager to use generative AI (gen AI) to enhance how healthcare stakeholders work and operate. More than 70% of healthcare organizations (payers, providers, healthcare services and technology groups) are pursuing or have already implemented gen AI capabilities, though many remain in proof-of-concept stages or take a wait-and-see approach.

---

## Current State of Gen AI Adoption

### Adoption Status
- **>70%** of surveyed healthcare organizations pursuing or implementing gen AI
- **Majority** in proof-of-concept stage
- Contemplating trade-offs among returns, risks, strategic priorities, governance, and maturity
- Consistent portion without plans or maintaining wait-and-see approach

### Barriers to Adoption
- **57%** of non-implementers cite risk concerns
- **29%** cite technology needs
- Risk concerns top the list regardless of organization type (payer, provider, HST)

---

## Implementation Approaches

### Partner vs. Build vs. Buy

**Among those implementing gen AI:**
- **59%** partnering with third-party vendors for customized solutions
- **24%** plan to build in-house
- **17%** expecting to buy off-the-shelf gen AI products

**Rationale for partnerships:** Cross-functional collaboration allows organizations to:
- Bring in outside technical expertise
- Create flexible, customizable solutions
- Reduce internal technical burden

**Among those not yet implementing:**
- **41%** intend to buy gen AI products
- May be driven by risk concerns and technology readiness gaps

### Technology Readiness
- Many healthcare organizations have limited tech readiness for gen AI deployment
- Infrastructure gaps in data and technology systems
- Need for capability building to validate and implement solutions

---

## Return on Investment (ROI)

### Current ROI Status
- **Small number** of respondents have implemented gen AI (early stages)
- **Among implementers:** Most haven't calculated ROI yet or awaiting measurable results
- **Positive outlook:** ~60% of those who implemented either:
  - Already seeing positive ROI, or
  - Expect to see positive ROI

### Key Point
ROI measurement remains critical for continued investment and expansion, though early results are encouraging.

---

## Gen AI's Highest-Value Opportunities

### Primary Value Areas

**Top Priority: Clinical and Clinician Productivity**
- Most respondents view this as highest-value opportunity
- Potential to improve healthcare provider efficiency significantly

**Secondary High-Value Areas:**
- **Patient and member engagement and experience:** Improving interactions with healthcare system
- **Administrative efficiency and effectiveness:** Streamlining operations
- **Quality of care and delivery:** Enhancing clinical outcomes

### Strategic Implication
Gen AI interest extends beyond clinical applications to broader patient care ecosystem, including:
- Operational improvements
- Patient engagement
- Experience enhancement
- Administrative workflows

---

## Roadblocks to Scaling Gen AI

### Top Challenges (in order of prevalence)

1. **Risk Concerns** (Most significant)
   - Privacy issues
   - Clinical outcome uncertainties
   - Regulatory compliance unknowns
   - Untested nature of technology
   - Substantial investment requirements
   - Unclear regulatory landscape

2. **Insufficient Capability**
   - Limited internal expertise
   - Need for specialized talent
   - Learning curve for new technologies

3. **Data and Technology Infrastructure Gaps**
   - Inadequate systems for gen AI implementation
   - Need for modernization
   - Integration challenges

4. **Proof of Value Challenges**
   - Difficulty demonstrating ROI
   - Lack of established use cases
   - Uncertainty about financial impact

### Critical Success Factors
- Strong governance and mitigation strategies essential
- Importance of establishing risk management frameworks
- Need for regulatory compliance assurance
- Clinical safety and quality assurance protocols

## Implementation Strategy Recommendations

### For Organizations Not Yet Implementing

**Considerations for different approaches:**

**Building In-House:**
- Best for organizations with strong technical capabilities
- Allows customization to specific needs
- Requires significant internal investment
- Longer development timeline

**Partnering with Vendors:**
- Leverages external expertise
- Provides customizable solutions
- Reduces internal technical burden
- Recommended for most healthcare organizations

**Buying Off-the-Shelf Solutions:**
- Most appropriate for simpler use cases
- Examples: Customer service applications
- Faster time-to-value
- May not fully address complex healthcare needs

### Phased Implementation Approach

**Initial Phase: Clinically Adjacent Applications**
- Focus on less risky, supporting functions
- Build confidence in technology
- Establish governance and risk management processes

**Next Phase: Core Clinical Applications**
- Move to direct clinical use as competencies mature
- Strong governance frameworks in place
- Demonstrated success in adjacent applications

## Governance and Risk Management

### Why Governance is Critical

Risk mitigation requires:
- Proactive governance processes and frameworks
- Guardrails to anticipate, identify, manage risks
- Risk categories to address:
  - **AI inaccuracy and bias:** Particularly critical with patient data
  - **Privacy:** Patient data protection
  - **Regulatory compliance:** HIPAA, FDA, other requirements
  - **Clinical safety:** Patient outcome integrity
  - **Ethical concerns:** Fairness, transparency, accountability

### Governance Framework Elements

1. **Risk Assessment:** Identify potential harms
2. **Mitigation Strategies:** Design controls and safeguards
3. **Oversight Processes:** Continuous monitoring
4. **Escalation Procedures:** Clear pathways for issues
5. **Compliance Tracking:** Ensure regulatory adherence

## Key Adoption Trends

### Trend 1: Partnerships Drive Implementation
- Strategic partnerships with third-party vendors becoming standard
- Enables technology access without building entire infrastructure
- Balances risk and capability considerations

### Trend 2: Caution with Risk
- Risk concerns driving decision-making
- Governance and mitigation top of mind
- Slower rollout of core clinical applications
- Testing and proving value in adjacent areas first

### Trend 3: Focus on Productivity First
- Clinical and administrative productivity most immediate value
- Patient engagement as secondary priority
- Operational efficiencies easier to measure and implement

### Trend 4: Technology Modernization Needed
- Infrastructure gaps limiting adoption
- Data and tech readiness critical prerequisite
- Investment in systems modernization ongoing

---

## Path Forward for Healthcare Organizations

### Immediate Actions

1. **Clarify Strategy**
   - Define gen AI role in organizational strategy
   - Identify highest-value use cases
   - Set ROI expectations

2. **Build Governance Capability**
   - Establish risk management frameworks
   - Design mitigation strategies
   - Create oversight processes

3. **Assess Technology Readiness**
   - Evaluate current data infrastructure
   - Identify modernization needs
   - Plan phased approach to upgrades

4. **Develop Partnerships**
   - Identify potential vendor partners
   - Evaluate build vs. buy vs. partner options
   - Begin proof-of-concept discussions

### Medium-Term Outlook

**Expectations as technology and competencies mature:**
- Expansion from adjacent to core clinical applications
- More sophisticated use cases as confidence builds
- Greater focus on patient-facing applications
- Integration with broader healthcare IT ecosystems

### Long-Term Vision

- Gen AI becoming standard tool across healthcare
- Significant improvements in clinician and administrative productivity
- Enhanced patient engagement and experience
- Better care quality and outcomes
- Optimized healthcare operations

## Research Methodology

### Survey Details
- **Q1 2024 survey:** 100 respondents (March 2024)
- **Q4 2023 survey:** 100 respondents (December 2023)
- **Initial survey:** 100 respondents (December 2023)
  - 40 payers, 40 providers, 20 HST companies

### Respondent Profile (Q1 2024)
- **33%** C-level executives
- **31%** from organizations with >$10 billion revenue
- Mix of geographic regions and healthcare segments

### Limitations
- Not comprehensive view of all healthcare stakeholders
- Early insights into gen AI potential
- Not predictive of future actions
- Qualitative in nature

---

## Key Takeaways for Healthcare Leaders

1. **Gen AI is relevant:** Over 70% of organizations actively pursuing technology
2. **Start with what you can control:** Focus on adjacent clinical applications first
3. **Governance is non-negotiable:** Build risk management frameworks before scaling
4. **Partnerships can accelerate:** Consider vendor partnerships for technical expertise
5. **Build your foundation:** Invest in data and technology infrastructure
6. **Measure value:** Establish clear ROI metrics from the start
7. **Plan for evolution:** Clinical applications will come as confidence grows
